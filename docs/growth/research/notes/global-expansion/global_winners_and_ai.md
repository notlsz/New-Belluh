## 1. TikTok/ByteDance: acquisition, spend, localization, India ban

### Takeaway
TikTok bought an existing Western user base (Musical.ly, merged Aug 2018), then poured in paid acquisition and ran content operations country by country. The India ban showed the downside: a single geopolitical event erased its largest market (about 200M users) overnight, and US incumbents absorbed the demand.

### Cited Findings
- Musical.ly was merged into TikTok's short-video platform; the article is dated 2 Aug 2018 — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/musical-ly-is-merging-with-tik-toks-short-video-platform) (headline)
- In 2018-19 TikTok passed the major US social media apps on one ranking (the article does not say which metric) — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/start-ups/article/2171702/tiktok-video-platform-vaults-ahead-major-us-social-media-apps-number) (headline)
- India banned TikTok and 58 other Chinese apps on 29 June 2020, after a border clash. TikTok had about 200M users in India, its biggest market. Individual creators lost millions of followers overnight (e.g., about 7M for one creator) — [NYT via WRAL, Mar 2024](https://www.wral.com/amp/21341758/)
- Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts absorbed most of the displaced demand, more than Indian rivals such as Moj did. YouTube had almost 500M monthly users in India and Instagram 362M — [NYT via WRAL](https://www.wral.com/amp/21341758/)
- ByteDance was reported to face a loss of up to about $6B from the India ban (a 2020 projection) — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2020/07/03/tiktok-parent-company-bytedance-faces-6-billion-loss-from-indias-ban-report.amp.html) (headline)
- After losing the creators' localized recommendation feed, small creators found it harder to get discovered — [NYT via WRAL](https://www.wral.com/amp/21341758/)

### Inferences
- The TikTok pattern is to buy distribution, subsidize growth with paid acquisition, and run content operations locally. Its weak point is geopolitics: a home-country association can trigger a ban that wipes out the largest market. AI companies with a strong national identity (DeepSeek, Doubao, and US labs in sovereignty-minded markets) face the same risk.
- In India, the incumbents that already had a similar format (Reels, Shorts) captured the demand, not local challengers. A market cleared by regulation tends to go to whoever already has distribution.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the commonly cited figure of about $1B in 2018 marketing spend. The mobiledevmemo/mdm.co article that examines it ("TikTok's billion-dollar secret that wasn't") was blocked by the fetch allowlist.
- I found no sources in this session on country-by-country content operations, creator funds, how TikTok won Japan, Indonesia, Brazil or Europe, or the Douyin/TikTok separation.

## 2. Shein and Temu: launch sequence, test-and-scale, ad spend, backlash

### Takeaway
Temu rolled out faster than any other consumer app in this research: US in Sept 2022, ANZ in Mar 2023, six European markets in Apr 2023, 49 countries by Jan 2024 and 90+ by Apr 2025. It paired that with Super Bowl and Meta ad spend. Regulation followed the scale: EU DSA findings, the end of the US de minimis exemption, and bans in Indonesia and Vietnam.

### Cited Findings
- Temu's launch sequence: US Sept 2022; Australia and NZ Mar 2023; France, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Spain and UK Apr 2023; South Africa 17 Jan 2024 (its 49th country); 90+ markets by Apr 2025 — [Temu Wikipedia mirror, citing Adweek, SCMP, Pandaily, Reuters](https://cliffipedia.org/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2025-08/Temu)
- Temu's first Super Bowl ad ran in Feb 2023, five months after launch. In Feb 2024 it offered $15M in giveaways across several Super Bowl LVIII spots (a giveaway figure, not the media cost) — [same, citing Adweek/CNBC](https://cliffipedia.org/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2025-08/Temu)
- Temu launched a Local Seller Program in the US in Mar 2024. By Jul 2025 it ran in the UK, France, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Australia and other markets, moving away from pure cross-border shipping — [same](https://cliffipedia.org/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2025-08/Temu)
- EU: BEUC (the European consumer organisation) filed a complaint in May 2024; the Commission opened a DSA investigation in Oct 2024 (illegal products, addictive design) and made a preliminary finding of breach in Jul 2025 — [same, citing Reuters/Politico](https://cliffipedia.org/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2025-08/Temu)
- US de minimis: a proposed rule to close the $800 exemption came in Sept 2024. After the exemption ended in May 2025, Temu stopped direct sales of China-shipped goods to US customers — [same, citing Reuters/Bloomberg/BBC](https://cliffipedia.org/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2025-08/Temu)
- Indonesia asked Apple and Google to block Temu in Oct 2024 to protect small merchants; Vietnam suspended it in Nov-Dec 2024; South Korea opened a privacy probe in 2024 — [same](https://cliffipedia.org/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2025-08/Temu)
- After the US tariff and de minimis change in 2025, Shein and Temu moved ad budgets to Europe, notably the UK and France — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/shein-temu-redirect-ad-budgets-europe-us-tariff-pressures) (headline); [TechNews.tw, May 2025](https://technews.tw/2025/05/06/shein-temu-eu-ads) (headline)

### Inferences
- Temu's expansion looks like a sprinkler built on paid acquisition: one product, launched country after country every few weeks, with ad budgets reallocated as regulators change the economics.
- Regulators acted once Temu was large and protecting local merchants became a political issue (Indonesia, Vietnam, EU). The faster a company grows across many markets, the sooner it meets that pushback.

### Gaps
- I did not research Shein's country sequence or its small-batch "test and reorder" data model in this session.
- I found no total ad-spend figures for Temu or Shein, and no confirmed media cost for the Super Bowl spots.

## 3. Netflix: 190-country launch, then local originals, PPP and mobile pricing

### Takeaway
Netflix launched in 130 new countries at once on 6 Jan 2016, reaching 190+. It then built local demand market by market with originals and price cuts; in India, regional content plus a mobile-only plan coincided with about 7x revenue growth in FY19.

### Cited Findings
- At CES in Jan 2016 Netflix announced an expansion to 130 new countries, including India, Russia and South Korea, putting the service in 190+ countries — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/netflix-expands-130-new-countries-including-india-russia-and-south-korea-and-more) (headline); [Netflix newsroom](https://about.netflix.com/news/netflix-is-now-available-around-the-world?data1=v2) (headline)
- India FY19: revenue grew 700%, from Rs 58 crore to Rs 466.7 crore (Veratech data). The growth is attributed to regional originals (Sacred Games) and a Rs 199/month mobile-only plan; an estimated 300-400k subscribers were added after the plan was announced, for an estimated 1.2M+ base. TechRadar dates the plan to Mar 2019 (I believe it was tested then and rolled out nationally in Jul 2019; unverified) — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/netflix-fy19-revenues-up-700-in-india-thanks-to-regional-content-and-mobile-plans)
- Netflix said Sacred Games season 2 was its most-watched show in India — [afaqs!](https://www.afaqs.com/news/media/sacred-games-s2-our-most-watched-show-in-india-netflix) (headline)
- APAC contributed more than 50% of Netflix's global subscriber growth in one reported period (date not verified) — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/apac-region-contributes-over-50-to-netflixs-global-subscriber-growth/) (headline)
- In 2026 analysis, local content dominates Netflix viewing in East Asia, while Australia relies on US imports — [Señal News, 28 Apr 2026](https://senalnews.com/en/data/local-content-dominates-east-asia-on-netflix-as-australia-leans-on-us-imports) (headline)
- Netflix's head said slow growth in India was "frustrating" — [Silverscreen India](https://silverscreenindia.com/streaming/news/netflix-head-says-platforms-slow-growth-in-india-is-frustrating/) (headline)

### Inferences
- Netflix's model is to switch on availability everywhere at once, which costs little for a digital product, and then spend on local content and pricing only where the data justifies it. This two-step model fits AI products well: ship the model everywhere, then invest in the markets with the most traction.
- India shows that availability alone does not drive adoption in price-sensitive markets; price points and format (mobile-only) matter.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Netflix's own 10-K or letters on how local originals affect subscriber growth, its non-English viewing share, or Squid Game and Money Heist data. Use Netflix's semiannual "What We Watched" engagement reports for this.

## 4. Duolingo, Canva, Spotify, Zoom, Figma, Stripe, WhatsApp, Telegram, Airbnb: localization approaches; Airbnb China exit

### Takeaway
Of this group, only Airbnb's China exit was researched in depth. Airbnb closed domestic China listings on 30 Jul 2022 after six years of effort. China was only about 1% of bookings, and it faced lockdowns, steep regulation, trouble hiring a China head, and the need for local engineers to build a China-specific product.

### Cited Findings
- Airbnb's domestic China business shut from 30 Jul 2022 — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8865050/airbnb-china-july30/) (headline)
- China was about 1% of Airbnb's business. Airbnb had pushed into China since 2016. Reasons given: pandemic lockdowns, steeper regulatory requirements, difficulty hiring a head of Airbnb China, and the need for local engineers to build a UX for Chinese consumers. It kept a China presence for outbound travel — [Inman, 23 May 2022](https://inman.com/2022/05/23/after-6-years-of-focus-airbnb-is-closing-its-business-in-china)

### Inferences
- China tends to require a separate product, a separate team and a separate regulatory track (Airbnb, Uber, and see the CAC filing regime in section 7). A foreign entrant that cannot staff all three should treat China as out of scope.

### Gaps
- Not researched in this session: language counts, local payments, PPP pricing and local teams for Duolingo, Canva, Spotify, Zoom, Figma, Stripe, WhatsApp and Telegram; and how WhatsApp won globally despite local rivals. ## 5. Uber's international playbook and failures

### Takeaway
Uber fought local champions with heavy subsidies, then exited by trading its local operations for equity: China to Didi (Aug 2016), Southeast Asia to Grab (Mar 2018), and Russia into a Yandex.Taxi joint venture (Jul 2017). It lost the markets but held about $12.5B in paper stakes at IPO.

### Cited Findings
- China: Uber sold to Didi in Aug 2016 for an 18.8% stake, later diluted to about 15.4%, worth about $7.95B at the end of 2018 — [TechCrunch citing Uber's S-1, Apr 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/11/uber-global-exits-billions/amp/)
- Southeast Asia: Uber sold to Grab in Mar 2018 for an initial stake of about 27-30%, diluted to about 23% and worth about $3.22B. Uber had spent about $700M in the region — [same](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/11/uber-global-exits-billions/amp/)
- Russia: the Yandex.Taxi joint venture formed in Jul 2017, with Uber holding about 38%, worth about $1.4B — [same](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/11/uber-global-exits-billions/amp/)
- Middle East: Uber agreed to buy Careem (15 markets) for $3.1B in Mar 2019, of which about $1.4B was cash. It bought a local champion rather than fighting it — [same](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/11/uber-global-exits-billions/amp/)
- Uber's minority stakes were collectively worth about $12.5B on paper, with at least $3B in gains — [same](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/11/uber-global-exits-billions/amp/)
- Coverage at the time framed the Southeast Asia sale as a "retreat from global markets" — [Al Jazeera, Mar 2018](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2018/03/uber-exits-southeast-asia-selling-operations-rival-180326124644472.html) (headline)

### Inferences
- Where a local champion has network effects that do not cross borders (ride-hailing liquidity in each city), attacking many markets at once drains cash. The workable exits were to merge into the champion for equity or to buy it outright.
- AI chat assistants have weak local network effects. Language quality, distribution and regulation work more like moats than city-level liquidity does, so the Uber "fight everywhere" lesson applies only partly.

### Gaps
- Uber's internal city-launcher playbook (city general manager model, launch checklists) was not researched in this session.

## 6. AI-specific: country usage, OpenAI for Countries and data residency, language gaps, local champions, who is winning

### Takeaway
ChatGPT dominates outside China and Russia: about 80% share in the US, about 75% in India and Germany, 70%+ in the UK, but only about 57% in Japan and about 48% in Korea (Jan 2026). China is a separate market led by Doubao (382M MAU, May 2026), with Qwen and DeepSeek behind. Usage tracks income per capita, and LLMs pay a measurable "token tax" in low-resource languages.

### Cited Findings

**ChatGPT scale and where its growth comes from**
- ChatGPT had 700M+ weekly active users by Jul 2025 (about 10% of world adults) and 2.5B+ messages a day. Growth was "significantly faster" in low- and middle-income countries (no figure given). About 70% of messages were non-work, up from 53% in Jun 2024 — [Digit, summarizing the OpenAI/NBER "How People Use ChatGPT" paper, Sept 2025](https://www.digit.in/?p=1154993); original: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/) (fetch failed)

**Share by country (Jan 2026, Similarweb + Sensor Tower)**
- ChatGPT share: US about 80%, India close to 75%, Germany approaching 75%, UK above 70%, Japan about 57% (Gemini second), South Korea about 48% (Gemini plus local Adot and Alice), Russia about 25% (GigaChat substantial), China minimal (Doubao large, DeepSeek meaningful) — [OfficeChai, 18 Mar 2026](https://officechai.com/ai/gemini-competitive-with-chatgpt-in-japan-and-south-korea-lags-in-us-and-india)
- a16z Top 100 (Aug 2025 edition): Gemini's traffic was about 12% of ChatGPT's on web but nearly half on mobile. Manus's top traffic source was Brazil, ahead of the US — [eWeek, 29 Aug 2025](https://www.eweek.com/news/top-100-consumer-ai-apps/)
- A 2026 ranking put DeepSeek fourth among AI apps worldwide as Chinese AI apps expanded overseas — [Tiger Brokers news](https://www.itiger.com/news/1120352644) (headline; date and methodology not verified)

**Anthropic Economic Index (Sept 2025)**
- The US has 21.6% of Claude.ai use, followed by India, then Brazil, Japan and South Korea at similar shares. Per working-age capita (the AUI), the top five are Israel, Singapore, Australia, New Zealand and South Korea — [Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/research/economic-index-geography)
- 1% higher GDP per capita is associated with 0.7% higher per-capita usage. Lower-use countries lean toward automation and higher-use countries toward augmentation. Brazilian users use Claude for translation and language learning about 6x the global average — [Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/research/economic-index-geography); full report: [arXiv 2511.15080](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15080)

**China: Doubao leads**
- China's AI-native apps reached 499M MAU in May 2026, up 85.4% year on year. Doubao 382M, Qwen 167M, DeepSeek 130M (QuestMobile H1 2026) — [TechNode, 14 Jul 2026](https://technode.com/2026/07/14/questmobile-chinas-ai-native-apps-reach-499-million-monthly-active-users/)
- Doubao remained China's top AI app while DeepSeek lost users — [Ground News aggregation](https://ground.news/article/bytedance-chatbot-doubao-still-chinas-top-ai-app-as-deepseek-loses-users) (headline)

**OpenAI for Countries and data residency**
- OpenAI extended data residency for enterprise customers to India and other Asian markets — [DatacenterDynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/openai-introduces-data-residency-in-asia/) (headline); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/openai-enables-data-residency-in-india-other-asian-countries) (headline)
- OpenAI launched free data residency for UAE business users in Nov 2025, later adding inference residency — [The National, 25 Nov 2025](https://thenational-the-national-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/future/technology/2025/11/25/openai-rolls-out-free-data-residency-service-for-business-users-in-the-uae) (headline); [Middle East AI News](https://www.middleeastainews.com/p/openai-now-offers-inference-residency) (headline)
- OpenAI announced "Stargate Norway," a European AI infrastructure hub — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/mn-MN/index/introducing-stargate-norway/) (headline)

**The language gap ("token tax")**
- On AfriMMLU (16 African languages, 10 LLMs), African languages trail English by about 25 points on average. Each extra token per word (fertility) costs up to 8-18 accuracy points, and fertility explains about 20-50% of the variance. Doubling token counts doubles inference cost and latency (e.g., GPT-4o $5/$20 rises to $10/$40 per 1M English-equivalent tokens). Reasoning models narrow the gap by 8-12 points — [arXiv 2509.05486, Sept 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05486v1)
- A 2026 paper audits the tokenization premium paid by underserved language communities — [arXiv 2608.09046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09046) (title only)

**Local champions**
- Aleph Alpha (Germany), once called Germany's answer to OpenAI, abandoned frontier LLM development to focus on enterprise applications. It pivoted in 2024 ([TechCrunch, Sept 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/05/german-llm-maker-aleph-alpha-pivots-to-ai-support), headline). On 24 Apr 2026, Cohere agreed to acquire it: Cohere shareholders get about 90% of the combined company, and Schwarz Group (Lidl's parent) will invest $600M in Cohere's next round. The pitch is sovereign AI for regulated European sectors, backed by a Canada-Germany Sovereign Technology Alliance — [Reuters via WHBL](https://whbl.com/2026/04/24/canadas-ai-startup-cohere-buys-germanys-aleph-alpha-to-expand-in-europe/)
- Mistral ranks among the top 5 AI traffic sources in France — [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/mistral-ai-traffic-research/) (headline; page content would not load)
- Russia: GigaChat (Sber) holds a substantial share and ChatGPT only about 25% — [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/gemini-competitive-with-chatgpt-in-japan-and-south-korea-lags-in-us-and-india)
- Korea: local assistants Adot (SK Telecom) and Alice share the market with ChatGPT (about 48%) and Gemini — [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/gemini-competitive-with-chatgpt-in-japan-and-south-korea-lags-in-us-and-india)

### Inferences
- Outside China and Russia, a strong global product beats local champions even in large non-English markets (India about 75%, Germany about 75%). Local champions win where the state or an ecosystem provides distribution: China (Douyin to Doubao, Alibaba to Qwen), Russia (Sber), and Korea (telecom bundles). Japan and Korea are the most contested markets that are open to foreign entrants.
- Model-only national champions struggle (Aleph Alpha). They survive by moving to sovereign enterprise or government deployments, or by consolidating (Cohere plus Aleph Alpha).
- Growth comes disproportionately from lower-income countries, but monetization tracks GDP per capita. For an AI company this means global-by-default free distribution, with paid go-to-market concentrated in high-AUI markets.
- The token tax is a real unit-cost problem in low-resource languages. Tokenizer and model quality in the target language decide whether a market can be served profitably.

### Gaps
- I did not verify current data for Sakana AI, ELYZA, Naver HyperCLOVA X, Kakao, Upstage, Sarvam, Krutrim, G42/Falcon, YandexGPT, Moonshot Kimi, or Le Chat user numbers.
- I could not get the exact country distribution of ChatGPT traffic (e.g., India's share of all ChatGPT visits) from a primary source. Semrush/Similarweb pages exist but were not fetched.
- I did not fetch OpenAI's "OpenAI for Countries" program page directly (launched May 2025, per my prior knowledge; unverified here).

## 7. Regulation affecting AI market entry

### Takeaway
Regulation now sets the order in which AI companies can enter markets. China requires pre-launch CAC filing (364+ new services filed in Jan-Aug 2026 alone). The EU AI Act's GPAI duties have applied since Aug 2025, while high-risk deadlines are slipping to Dec 2027 and Aug 2028. Korea's AI Basic Act took effect on 22 Jan 2026 and requires a local representative above certain thresholds. Japan's 2025 law promotes AI rather than restricting it, and India's DPDP Rules phase in through May 2027.

### Cited Findings
- **EU AI Act:** In force 1 Aug 2024; prohibitions and AI literacy from 2 Feb 2025; GPAI obligations from 2 Aug 2025. High-risk deadlines were originally 2 Aug 2026 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2027 (Annex I). The Digital Omnibus postpones them to 2 Dec 2027 and 2 Aug 2028; provisional political agreement came on 6 May 2026 and Council confirmed it on 13 May 2026, with formal adoption pending — [Gibson Dunn, 27 May 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- **Korea AI Basic Act:** Effective 22 Jan 2026. A foreign operator must appoint a domestic representative if it meets any of these: prior-year revenue of KRW 1T or more (about $662M), AI-service revenue of KRW 10B or more (about $6.6M), or 1M+ average daily Korean users. Generative AI must be labeled, with notices in Korean in practice. High-impact AI needs risk management and human oversight; there are extra duties above 10^26 FLOPs. Fines are generally deferred during a grace period of at least one year — [US ITA / trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/south-korea-ai-basic-act)
- **China:** Generative AI services must file with the CAC under the 2023 Interim Measures. 346 services had registered by Apr 2025 ([CGTN, 9 Apr 2025](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2025-04-09/346-generative-AI-services-registered-with-China-s-cyber-authority-1CqpnxsMee4/share_amp.html), headline). The 2026 batches were 48 (Jan-Feb), 72 (Mar-Apr), 120 (May-Jun) and 124 (Jul-Aug, released 14 Sept 2026) — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/43685-cyberspace-administration-issued-filing-information-for-generative-ai-services-for-july-to-august-2026)
- **Japan:** The Act on Promotion of R&D and Application of AI-Related Technologies (Act No. 53 of 2025) passed the lower house on 24 Apr 2025 and the upper house on 28 May 2025, and took effect on 4 Jun 2025 (some chapters later) — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/13446-act-on-promotion-of-research-and-development-and-application-of-artificial-intelligence-related-technologies). That page does not describe the law's nature; I understand it to be a framework law without penalties, but did not confirm this here.
- **India DPDP:** The Rules were notified on 13 Nov 2025. Procedural provisions applied from 14 Nov 2025; consent-manager provisions follow in about Nov 2026; all substantive duties (consent, notice, children's data, cross-border transfer, breach notification) apply from about May 2027. Cross-border transfer is allowed by default, but the government can blacklist countries, and Significant Data Fiduciaries can be required to keep specified data in India. Fines reach INR 2.5B (about $28M) for security failures; logs must be retained for 1 year — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/s-r-data-india-s-digital-personal-data-protection-regime-takes-effect)

### Inferences
- The regulatory cost of entry now runs, from highest to lowest: China (pre-approval, effectively closed to foreign models), then the EU (GPAI documentation now, high-risk duties from 2027-28), then Korea (local representative and labeling, enforcement deferred), then India (light until May 2027), then Japan (promotion-oriented). A company launching in many markets at once can enter Japan, India and Korea quickly, and should plan the EU compliance build for 2027.
- Data residency has become a standard enterprise feature (OpenAI in the UAE, India and Asia). Without it, a vendor loses regulated buyers even where the law does not strictly require it.

### Gaps
- GDPR enforcement actions against AI companies (e.g., Italy's Garante versus OpenAI and DeepSeek) were not researched in this session.

## 8. Sprinkler vs waterfall, and the payoff from localization

### Takeaway
The best evidence for localization's payoff is CSA Research's 2020 survey (8,709 consumers in 29 countries): 76% prefer product information in their own language, and 40% will not buy from sites in other languages. I found no rigorous public study showing that a simultaneous global launch outperforms a sequenced one for apps or AI. The case evidence points to a hybrid: availability everywhere, investment sequenced to traction.

### Cited Findings
- CSA Research "Can't Read, Won't Buy" (B2C), 7 Jul 2020: 8,709 consumers in 29 countries; 76% prefer buying products with information in their native language; 40% won't buy from websites in other languages — [CSA Research](https://csa-research.com/blogs-events/csa-in-the-media/press-releases/consumers-prefer-their-own-language)
- Case evidence for the sprinkler approach: Netflix launched 130 countries in one day (Jan 2016) — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/netflix-expands-130-new-countries-including-india-russia-and-south-korea-and-more) (headline); Temu reached 49 countries in about 16 months and 90+ in about 31 months — [Temu mirror](https://cliffipedia.org/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2025-08/Temu); ChatGPT reached 700M weekly users with growth fastest in lower-income countries — [Digit](https://www.digit.in/?p=1154993)
- Case evidence for the cost of attacking everywhere: Uber spent about $700M in Southeast Asia before selling to Grab — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/11/uber-global-exits-billions/amp/); Airbnb spent six years in China and left at about 1% of bookings — [Inman](https://inman.com/2022/05/23/after-6-years-of-focus-airbnb-is-closing-its-business-in-china)

### Inferences
- For software with near-zero marginal cost per country and weak local network effects (streaming catalogs, AI assistants), a sprinkler launch is cheap and captures organic demand. The expensive items are local content, local payments and pricing, compliance, and paid acquisition, and those should go out in waves to the markets that show traction.
- For businesses with local network effects or physical operations (ride-hailing, lodging, marketplaces), simultaneous attacks on entrenched local champions have usually ended in exits or mergers.
- For AI specifically, language quality (the token tax), price per user (income-linked adoption) and regulatory gates (China, the EU) determine which waves come first.

### Gaps
- I found no app-store localization uplift studies (Sensor Tower or data.ai metadata-localization A/B results) in this session.
- I found no quantitative academic comparison of sprinkler and waterfall launch outcomes for apps; the research basis is mostly case studies and older diffusion literature (e.g., Kalish, Mahajan and Muller, 1995), which was not fetched.
