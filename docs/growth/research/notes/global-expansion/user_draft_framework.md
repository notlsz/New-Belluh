# User-supplied draft framework (from the user, Nov 1 2026). Treat as the user's own thinking to build on and check, not as verified research. Its cited sources (irejournals, ijrpr, lex-localis, stratrix) are low-quality; do not rely on them. Verify any factual claims against the other notes.

Thesis: not "annex every market at once" but parallel market selection with sequenced conquest. Standardize the global AI core; give a few priority markets local autonomy over data, distribution, pricing, compliance.

Unit-of-value redesign table: Taobao (chat, bargaining, trust), Flipkart (cash on delivery), Grab (cash, bikes, super-app), Gojek (motorcycles, food/logistics), WeChat (messaging -> payments/identity), KFC China (menu, breakfast, membership, local ownership). Pattern: locals remove a structural friction (payment, trust, infrastructure, language, regulation, status) the global player treats as secondary.

Playbook steps proposed:
1. Invariant core (standardize): model/routing layer, security/eval/safety, agent primitives, brand promise, developer platform. Localize surface and constraints: language/dialect/honorifics, local documents/tax/legal, payments/pricing/procurement/residency, distribution (WhatsApp, LINE, Kakao, WeChat, Telegram, telcos, resellers, gov cloud), trust signals.
2. Choose markets by friction, not GDP. Score: workflow pain, incumbent weakness, distribution access, regulatory feasibility, payment/procurement reality, talent density, defensibility.
3. Three-tier portfolio: beachheads (2-4, heavy), fast-follow (5-10, medium), platform markets (many, light self-serve/API). Simultaneous at platform layer, concentrated at account/workflow layer.
4. Market-in-a-box kit: local eval dataset, country templates, compliance pack, payments/invoicing, local case studies, partner playbook, pricing ladder, onboarding to first value <10 min, local human support. Goal: market #8 costs 30-50% of market #1 (this number is an assertion, not sourced).
5. Win the trust last mile; localize risk messaging: Germany/Japan (data protection, precision, audit), US (ROI, speed, integrations), India/SEA/LatAm/Africa (mobile, low bandwidth, local languages, affordability, human support), China/Gulf (sovereignty, local deployment), regulated sectors (citations, provenance, human review).
Market plans: US (self-serve bottom-up, ROI, integrations), Western Europe (GDPR, residency, augment not replace, SIs, regulated verticals), Japan/Korea (local leaders, polished localization, reference customers, white-glove, local cloud), China (partner/license or separate entity; WeChat/Alipay; decide early serve/avoid/separate), India (Indic languages, low-cost tiers, UPI, WhatsApp, mid-range Android), SEA (separate countries, WhatsApp/LINE/Zalo, telcos/banks, merchant agents), LatAm (WhatsApp-native SMB agents, Pix/boleto/installments, partners), MEA (Arabic dialects/French/Swahili/Hausa/Amharic, voice-first, low connectivity, telcos/govs, sovereign deployment).
Org: global centralization (research, safety, platform, eval, brand, pricing architecture, security/legal, partner economics) + regional pods owning activation/retention/revenue.
Cadence: Phase 1 Market Reality Map (incumbent workflows, competitors, objections, payment path, regulation, trust, partner, first 20 design partners); Phase 2 beachhead with one narrow job (examples: WhatsApp collections agent for Brazilian SMBs; German mid-market legal review; Hindi/English tutoring; Japanese insurance broker claims; Indonesian Shopee merchant support), 20-50 design partners, metrics (time to first value, WAU, task completion, correction rate, cost per resolved task, NRR, referral rate); Phase 3 local monopoly (10x on one workflow); Phase 4 clone the motion not the product.
Priority order: workflow outcome, native language quality, trust/safety, distribution, payment fit, local partnerships, price architecture, brand last.
Anti-playbook: localization as translation; too many countries with generic page; English-first quality assumptions; ignoring residency/procurement; competing on model IQ vs workflow reliability; self-serve in relationship markets; brand ads before PMF; HQ controlling local decisions; entering where a local owns distribution and trust; one global price.
Formula: global model and platform + local workflow ownership + local trust infrastructure + local distribution + local pricing.
