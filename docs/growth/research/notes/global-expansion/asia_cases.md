## 1. Grab vs Uber (Southeast Asia)

### Takeaway
Uber did not get "beaten out" of SEA in the sense of being driven to zero. It sold the business to Grab in March 2018 for a 27.5% stake, a capital-efficiency exit after a costly subsidy war. Grab's local playbook (cash, motorbikes, then payments and lending) grew into a profitable super-app: FY2025 was its first full year of net profit ($200M). The user's "over 70% unbanked" claim is wrong for the region as a whole. It roughly fits Indonesia, the Philippines and Vietnam around 2011 to 2014, but not Malaysia, Thailand or Singapore.

### Cited Findings
- Deal (announced Mar 26 2018, Singapore time): Grab acquired all of Uber's SEA operations, including Uber Eats, across Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam. Uber received a 27.5% stake in Grab and CEO Dara Khosrowshahi joined Grab's board. Uber Eats was folded into GrabFood, and Uber ride-hailing support ended within two weeks **[verified]** — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/blog/2018/03/25/uber-sells-southeast-asia-business-grab-return-27-5-stake/)
- Wire coverage framed the sale as coming "after costly battle" **[search-title only]** — [NST/Reuters](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/business/home/2018/03/349460/uber-agrees-sell-southeast-asia-business-grab-after-costly-battle); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2018/03/uber-exits-southeast-asia-selling-operations-rival-180326124644472.html)
- **Unbanked fact-check (World Bank Global Findex):** Indonesian adults with an account were 20% in 2011, 36% in 2014 and 48.9% in 2017 **[verified]** — [NextBillion on Findex 2017](https://nextbillion.net/news/global-findex-indonesia-leads-east-asia-pacific-financial-inclusion-progress). So Indonesia was about 80% unbanked in 2011 and 64% in 2014, when Grab and Gojek were scaling. "Over 70%" fits Indonesia only in roughly 2011 to 2013.
- Philippines: account ownership reported at about 34% in Findex 2017, rising to 51% in Findex 2021 **[search-title only]** — [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2022/06/30/2191918/51-pinoy-adults-have-financial-accounts)
- Findex 2021 chapter on account ownership (country tables) — [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/globalfindex/brief/the-global-findex-database-2021-chapter-1-ownership-of-accounts). Findex 2025 was released in 2025 — [World Bank PolicyResearchTalk Sept 2025](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/a4aaae5a017a5bea7922686af0ed3636-0050022025/original/PolicyResearchTalk-GlobalFindex-September2025.pdf). I did not extract SEA country figures for 2021 or 2025.
- **Grab FY2025** (release dated Feb 12 2026): **[verified]** — [Grab press release](https://www.grab.com/sg/press/others/grab-reports-fourth-quarter-and-2025-results-with-first-full-year-net-profit/)
  - Revenue $3.37B, up 20% YoY
  - Profit for the year $200M, its first full-year net profit and a $358M improvement
  - Adjusted EBITDA $500M, up from $313M in 2024
  - On-Demand GMV $22.1B, up 21%
  - "Crossing 50 million" monthly transacting users
  - Net loan book $1.18B, more than double the $536M a year earlier (gross $1.278B)
  - Digital-bank deposits at GXS (Singapore) and GXBank (Malaysia) of $1.6B
  - 2028 targets: $1.5B adjusted EBITDA and 80% adjusted FCF conversion; new $500M buyback
- Malaysia remains Grab's top market by revenue **[search-title only]** — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/03/268025-se-asia-superapp-grab-2025-revenue-hits-record-3-37bn-as-malaysia-stays-top-market/)

### Inferences
- Decisive decisions and sequencing:
  1. Taxi-hailing first (MyTeksi/GrabTaxi, from 2012), working with existing taxi fleets
  2. Cash payments and GrabBike (motorbike taxis), localized per country
  3. Payments (GrabPay)
  4. Absorbing Uber in 2018 and using its Uber Eats base to scale GrabFood
  5. Lending and digital banks (GXS/GXBank)
  6. Cost discipline toward profit by 2025

  Steps 1, 2 and 5 are **[unverified: prior knowledge]**; the 2018 deal, Uber Eats into GrabFood, and the bank, loan and profit figures are verified above.
- What Uber got wrong: it treated SEA as one of many subsidized fronts, while Grab focused on one region. Its card-first, car-only template did not fit cities dominated by motorbikes and cash (inference).
- Cultural or behavioral advantages: cash habits and motorbike commuting. Structural advantages: capital from SoftBank, Didi and Toyota (**[unverified: prior knowledge]**), plus regional regulatory relationships.
- Transferable lesson: the local winner needs to outlast the global player, not drive it out. The global player exits when the market stops being a good use of its capital, so it pays to own the payment layer and keep regulators close while the global player is fighting on several fronts at once.

## 2. Gojek (Indonesia)

### Takeaway
Gojek began as an ojek (motorbike-taxi) call-centre business and became a super-app (GoRide, GoFood, GoSend, GoPay), then merged with Tokopedia into GoTo in May 2021, a deal reported at about $18B. Answer to the user's question: Gojek did not defeat Uber. Uber left Indonesia by selling to Grab in 2018, and Grab, not Gojek, was the acquirer.

### Cited Findings
- Gojek and Tokopedia announced their merger into GoTo Group on May 17 2021 **[search-title only]** — [AP via Click2Houston](https://www.click2houston.com/business/2021/05/17/indonesias-gojek-tokopedia-to-merge-as-goto-group/)

### Inferences
- **[unverified: prior knowledge]** Gojek was founded in 2010 by Nadiem Makarim as a call centre for ojek drivers, with the app launched in January 2015. It relied on Indonesia's ojek culture and a low share of bank accounts (36% in 2014, verified above). GoPay was an early wallet. The later 2023 to 2024 sale of Tokopedia control to TikTok/ByteDance is relevant but unverified here.
- Transferable lesson: formalize an informal behavior people already have (ojek, cash couriers) rather than importing a foreign format.

## 3. Flipkart vs Amazon India

### Takeaway
Walmart bought about 77% of Flipkart for about $16B in May 2018. India's FDI rules restrict foreign-owned e-commerce firms to a marketplace model with no inventory. Those rules constrained Amazon and the Walmart-owned Flipkart alike, and the December 2018 tightening hit both. Recent data shows Flipkart leading on user growth, but I could not verify a current market-share figure.

### Cited Findings
- Walmart buys 77% of Flipkart for about $16B (May 9 2018) **[search-title only, multiple outlets]** — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/deals/story/flipkart-walmart-deal-77-percent-stake-16-billion-india-valuation-buys-107437-2018-05-09); [Business Standard/Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/reuters/walmart-buys-controlling-stake-in-india-s-flipkart-for-16-billion-118050900894_1.html)
- The December 26 2018 FDI Press Note tightened marketplace rules and drew commentary on its effect on Amazon and Flipkart **[search-title only]** — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/changes-fdi-norms-e-commerce-impacted-giants-like-amazon-flipkart/); [IndiaCorpLaw, Jan 12 2019](https://indiacorplaw.in/2019/01/12/dipps-revised-fdi-policy-e-commerce-changing-rules-game/); [iPleaders on Amazon restructuring](https://blog.ipleaders.in/amazon-restructured-comply-new-fdi-rules-e-commerce/)
- CLSA (via Hindu BusinessLine, May 16 2026): Flipkart added 26.8M weekly active users year to date, against 10.6M for peers; Meesho lost 5.9M WAUs **[verified]** — [Pulse/BusinessLine](https://www.pulse.bot/ecommerce/news/flipkart-widens-lead-over-amazon-meesho-in-ecommerce-user-growth-clsa-78858606-686b-44eb-9e07-a24a0ec29686/)
- Headlines claim Flipkart has a 50 to 60% GMV share **[search-title only; underlying report not verified]** — [askfuzz](https://askfuzz.ai/discover/news/companies/flipkart-leads-india-e-commerce-with-50-60-gmv-share)
- Redseer reported Meesho overtaking Amazon on festive-sale order volume, and Amazon disputed the report **[search-title only]** — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/festive-sales-report-meesho-zooms-past-amazon-flipkart-leads-with-highest-volume-of-sales-2458.htm); [Indian Retailer](https://www.indianretailer.com/news/amazon-dismisses-redseers-latest-report)

### Inferences
- **[unverified: prior knowledge — user's claims, not confirmed this session]**
  - Flipkart is often credited with popularizing cash on delivery in India around 2010; it did not invent COD.
  - It built its own logistics arm, Ekart.
  - Big Billion Days began in October 2014.
  - Press Note 3 (2016) allowed 100% FDI only in the marketplace model and banned inventory.
  - Press Note 2 (Dec 2018, effective Feb 1 2019) barred marketplaces from selling products of vendors they hold equity in and from exclusive deals.
  - Amazon has announced cumulative India investment commitments in the tens of billions.
- Important nuance for the narrative: after 2018, "local versus global" in India is really Walmart versus Amazon, and both are bound by the same FDI rules. Flipkart's edge comes from its early start, regional and fashion depth, and logistics, not from regulatory favor over Amazon. Regulation protects domestic offline retailers, not Flipkart.
- Meesho, which targets value-conscious and smaller-town buyers on a zero-commission model, is the newer local challenger.

### Gaps
- An authoritative current market-share figure (Bernstein, Redseer, etc.): not verified.
- COD first-mover claim, Ekart and Big Billion Days dates, Amazon's investment totals, and the exact Press Note numbers and dates: unverified this session.

## 4. Paytm, PhonePe/UPI vs Google Pay and WhatsApp Pay

### Takeaway
UPI is public infrastructure run by NPCI, which turned payments into an interoperable rail where apps compete on distribution. The Walmart-owned PhonePe and Google Pay together dominate it. Their combined share fell below 80% (79%) for the first time in May 2026, while Paytm shrank after RBI's January 2024 action against Paytm Payments Bank. NPCI's 30% per-app cap has been deferred repeatedly.

### Cited Findings
- May 2026 NPCI data **[verified]** — [Outlook Business, Jun 17 2026](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/phonepe-google-pay-combined-upi-market-share-drops-below-80-for-first-time)
  - PhonePe and Google Pay combined: 79%, down from 86% in May 2024
  - PhonePe, Google Pay and Paytm combined: 87%, down from 95.2% in January 2024
  - Navi plus super.money: 5.5%
  - BHIM: 1%
  - WhatsApp Pay grew but no share figure was given
  - The 30% cap was deferred by two years on Dec 31 2024; at publication its enforcement was about six months away
- PhonePe's UPI share was 46.46% in June 2025 **[search-title only]** — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/start-ups/phonepe-market-share-upi-46-46-percent-june-125071101033_1.html)
- UPI processed 21,860 crore (about 218.6B) transactions in FY26 **[search-title only]** — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/upi-processes-21860-crore-transactions-in-fy26-peak-growth-seen-in-dec-jan-months-523412-2026-03-31)
- RBI on Jan 31 2024 barred Paytm Payments Bank from accepting deposits, top-ups and wallet loads after Feb 29 2024; the deadline was later extended to Mar 15 **[search-title only]** — [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/companies/news/rbi-bars-paytm-payments-bank-from-accepting-deposits-from-february-29-124013100875_1.html); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/31/rbi-paytm-payments-bank-penalty/embed/); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=443363)

### Inferences
- Sequencing: Paytm's wallet boomed after demonetization (Nov 8 2016, **[unverified: prior knowledge]**). UPI (launched 2016) then commoditized wallets, and the winners were the apps with the best UPI UX and distribution: PhonePe (Flipkart's, later Walmart's) and Google Pay (Tez, 2017). That means a global player (Google) can win when the state provides the rail. Paytm's regulatory and compliance failure in 2024 was self-inflicted.
- Lesson: when a public rail exists, compete on distribution and trust, not on owning the rail. A local champion's regulatory risk can outweigh its home advantage.

### Gaps
- Paytm's exact current UPI share (about 8% by subtraction from the Outlook figures; not reported directly) and WhatsApp Pay's share.
- Whether the 30% cap deadline was deferred again in late 2026.

## 5. Korea: Kakao, Naver, Cyworld

### Takeaway
Korea is the strongest case of local platforms holding off global ones. Naver still holds about 63% of search (2025) against Google's 29.6%. KakaoTalk has 48.2M monthly users in a country of about 51M. Cyworld, the pre-Facebook leader, lost because of late mobile adoption, reliance on dotori revenue, conglomerate slowness and a Korea-only scope, not mainly because of Facebook.

### Cited Findings

  | Engine | 2022 | 2024 | 2025 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Naver | 61.20% | 58.14% | 62.86% |
  | Google | — | about 33.0% | 29.55% |
  | Bing | — | 2.91% | 3.12% |
  | Daum | — | 3.72% | 2.94% |

  Naver credits its AI Briefing service for the 2025 gain.
- In 2024, Google and Bing together took over 40% of Korea's web search **[search-title only]** — [Daum](https://v.daum.net/v/20240812113902779)
- KakaoTalk had 48.19M MAUs in August 2025, the most in Korea. Instagram had 27.41M and Band 17.08M (Wiseapp Retail, via Yonhap) **[verified]** — [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/companies/20250924/kakaotalk-leads-in-users-tiktok-lite-in-usage-time-data)
- Cyworld **[verified]** — [Korea Herald, Dec 2021](https://www.koreaherald.com/view.php?ud=20211215000870)
  - Started in 1999 as a KAIST graduation project
  - 32M users at peak, out of about 50M people
  - Acquired by SK Communications in 2003 and tied to the NateOn messenger, later eclipsed by KakaoTalk
  - Dotori ("acorn") virtual currency for decorating the minihompy; dotori sales were 43% of SK Communications' Q2 2009 profit
  - Decline: Korea-only, late to mobile, slow conglomerate decisions, the minihompy never moved to smartphones
- In 2008 Cyworld was already "losing luster to MySpace, Facebook" **[search-title only]** — [Korea Times 2008](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/companies/20080924/cyworld-losing-luster-to-myspace-facebook)
- Its revival went offline in 2023 **[search-title only]** — [Daum](https://v.daum.net/v/20230801185301160)

### Inferences
- Naver's moat **[unverified: prior knowledge]**:
  - It launched Knowledge iN (Q&A) in 2002, when there was little Korean-language web content, so it created its own corpus
  - Portal design that blends news, blogs, Cafe and shopping into results
  - Closed content (Naver Blog and Cafe are poorly indexed by Google)
- Google's gains to about 33% in 2024 show the moat is eroding, with mobile/Android defaults and younger users moving to Google and YouTube. Naver's 2025 rebound with AI Briefing shows AI search could re-entrench a local incumbent.
- KakaoTalk **[unverified: prior knowledge]**: launched March 2010, free and early on iPhone when SMS was paid, then used its social graph to expand into games, Kakao Pay, Kakao T and KakaoBank. WhatsApp and LINE never gained traction in Korea.
- Lesson: in a language-specific market, local content and social graphs compound. The local winner keeps winning by owning the content and the graph, and by moving first on each platform shift (mobile, then AI). Cyworld lost to a local player (Kakao) that moved first on mobile.

### Gaps
- Kakao's launch date and messenger share against WhatsApp and LINE: not verified this session.
- Naver's market-share history for the 2000s.

## 6. Japan: LINE, Yahoo Japan, Mixi

### Takeaway
LINE launched on June 23 2011, three months after the Tohoku earthquake, built by NHN Japan (part of Korea's Naver). Stickers followed in October 2011. Mixi held about 80% of Japanese social networking at its peak. It lost ground to Facebook from 2011 to 2012, a shift tied to the earthquake, Facebook's real-name model gaining acceptance, and Mixi's closed design (it required a Japanese mobile number).

### Cited Findings
- LINE launched on June 23 2011 and stickers arrived in October 2011 **[verified]** — [LY Corp history](https://www.lycorp.co.jp/en/company/history/line/)
  - Thailand entity May 2014; Taiwan entity June 2014
  - LINE Pay launched in Taiwan in August 2015 and in Thailand in January 2016; LINE MAN in May 2016
  - Listed on the NYSE and TSE in July 2016; delisted in December 2020
- Origin: after the earthquake damaged phone networks, NHN Japan (a Naver subsidiary) staff built LINE, launched about three months later. It reached 50M users in under a year **[verified]** — [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/how-japans-most-popular-messaging-app-emerged-from-the-2011-earthquake/articleshow/28713054.cms)
- Mixi **[verified]** — [Eurasia Review, Jul 2012](https://www.eurasiareview.com/03072012-facebook-takes-off-in-japan-as-local-network-mixi-struggles/)
  - About 80% of the Japanese SNS market at its peak
  - Facebook went from under 2% of Japan's online population in early 2011 to about 30% by mid-2012
  - Japanese sites let users mask their identities
  - A Japanese mobile number was required to open an account
  - The earthquake pushed people toward social networks
  - The source's "lost almost 60 million users" figure is implausible for a site with about 27M registered users; treat it as an error
- A 2008 debate on Mixi and anonymity **[search-title only]** — [Global Voices](https://globalvoices.org/2008/06/02/japan-mixi-and-anonymity/)
- Mixi pivoted to mobile games (Monster Strike) **[search-title only]** — [TechNews TW](https://technews.tw/2014/11/10/mixi-downs-in-social-rises-in-mobile-game/)

### Inferences
- Invite-only: Mixi required invitations until 2010 **[unverified: prior knowledge]**. That and the mobile-number gate limited growth just as smartphones arrived.
- Mixi's loss shows that cultural fit (anonymity) is not durable once a shock (the earthquake) and a platform shift (smartphones) change behavior. Note the parallel: Japan's winning messenger, LINE, came from a Korean company and beat WhatsApp, a "foreign but Asian, locally built" outcome.
- Yahoo Japan **[unverified: prior knowledge]**: a 1996 joint venture of SoftBank and Yahoo, a portal model, and for years Google's search engine powered its results under a 2010 deal. It merged with LINE as Z Holdings in 2021 and became LY Corporation in October 2023.
- Lesson: localize the product, not only the language. LINE's stickers fit Japanese kawaii and indirect-communication culture (inference).

### Gaps
- Yahoo Japan versus Google search shares, LINE Yahoo merger dates, and LINE MAU figures for Japan, Taiwan and Thailand: not verified this session.

## 7. Rakuten vs Amazon Japan

### Takeaway
Rakuten's merchant-mall model (merchants control their storefronts) and its points currency across 70+ services counter Amazon's Prime. It held an estimated 23.3% of Japanese domestic e-commerce in 2022, but that source is secondary and weakly sourced.

### Cited Findings
- From GLOBIS Europe, May 2025 (a secondary source that cites few primary sources) **[verified that the article says this]** — [GLOBIS](https://globis.eu/amazon-vs-rakuten-how-loyalty-points-beat-prime-in-japan/)
  - Rakuten Ichiba launched May 1 1997 with 13 merchants and a flat fee of ¥50,000 a month
  - Merchants control storefronts and customer communication; no private label
  - Over 4 trillion Super Points issued as of 2024, at ¥1 each, usable across 70+ services
  - Over 70% of users use multiple services
  - Over 100M members, against about 18.6M Amazon Prime users (an unnamed estimate)
  - About 23.3% e-commerce share in 2022

### Inferences
- Lesson: the local player wins by turning loyalty into a cross-service currency (card, bank, mobile, travel) that a single-vertical foreign entrant can't match. Merchant control suits Japanese retailers' emphasis on presentation and customer relationships.

### Gaps
- Primary-source GMV for Rakuten versus Amazon Japan (company IR or METI): not found. Amazon Japan's share may now match or exceed Rakuten's. Unverified.

## 9. Careem vs Uber (Middle East)

### Takeaway
Careem, founded in 2012, won share with cash payments and local adaptation in markets such as Egypt and Pakistan while Uber was card-only at first. Uber bought it for $3.1B ($1.7B in convertible notes and $1.4B cash), announced on Mar 26 2019 and closed in early 2020, and kept the Careem brand.

### Cited Findings
- Deal terms **[verified]** — [WLOS/AP](https://wlos.com/news/nation-world/uber-acquires-mideast-competitor-careem-for-31-billion)
  - $3.1B total: $1.7B convertible notes plus $1.4B cash
  - Announced Mar 26 2019, expected to close in Q1 2020
  - Careem launched in 2012 by two ex-McKinsey consultants; 100+ cities in 15 countries
  - Brand and app kept separate, run by its founders
  - Careem offered cash where Uber "initially accepted only credit cards," which mattered in Egypt and Pakistan
  - It explored buses for low-income riders in Egypt
- Closing **[search-title only]** — [MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/UBER-TECHNOLOGIES-INC-57860975/news/Uber-Technologies-Inc-NYSE-UBER-completed-the-acquisition-of-Careem-Networks-FZ-LLC-from-Al-Tayy-34031428/); [Uber 10-Q FY2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315120000022/fy2020q1financialstate.htm)

### Inferences
- Women's-safety features are **[unverified: prior knowledge]**. Saudi Arabia, where women could not drive until June 2018, was a core rider base, and Careem later recruited female "captains."
- Here the global player won by acquiring rather than exiting, the mirror image of Grab. In 2023 Careem's super-app was spun off, with e& taking 50.03% **[unverified: prior knowledge]**.
