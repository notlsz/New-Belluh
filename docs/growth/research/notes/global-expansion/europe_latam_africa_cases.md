# Local vs Global: Europe, LatAm, Middle East/Central Asia, Africa, plus global-brand wins and failures abroad

Research date: 1 Nov 2026. Each finding below was checked against a fetched page unless marked otherwise. Items I could not verify on a fetched page are listed under Gaps, flagged "UNVERIFIED (background knowledge)" so the writer knows not to present them as sourced fact.

## Q1. Rocket Internet / Samwer brothers: Alando, CityDeal, Zalando, StudiVZ

### Takeaway
The Samwer pattern holds up: copy a proven US model, launch in Germany or Europe before the US original arrives, then sell to it or outlast it. But the user's specific numbers need fixing. eBay never disclosed the Alando price. CityDeal took about 6 months to sell, not "100 days". Zalando's famous 100-day free returns were cut to 30 days in its biggest markets from 2024 to Jan 2025. StudiVZ is the counter-case: a local clone that won early, then lost to the global network once Facebook localized.

### Cited Findings
**Alando (eBay clone)**
- Alando launched in March 1999. eBay announced the acquisition on 22 June 1999, about 100 days later, and "declined to disclose financial terms." — [CNN Money, 22 Jun 1999](https://money.cnn.com/1999/06/22/technology/ebay/)
- FACT-CHECK: "~100 days after launch" is confirmed (March to 22 June 1999). The "~$43M" price is NOT in the primary report. It's a figure that circulates in secondary sources and books (e.g., Die Paten des Internets, [getAbstract summary](https://www.getabstract.com/de/zusammenfassung/die-paten-des-internets/22365)), but I could not confirm it on a fetched page. Present it as "reported", not confirmed.

**CityDeal (Groupon clone)**
- CityDeal was founded in Berlin in November 2009 and acquired by Groupon in May 2010. It was co-branded under the Groupon name and now serves 10 countries. — [Wikipedia: Groupon MyCityDeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupon_MyCityDeal)
- Groupon founder Andrew Mason publicly explained why he bought "the clone" (17 May 2010). — [deutsche-startups.de](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2010/05/17/warum-kauft-groupon-den-klon-citydeal-gruender-andrew-mason-nimmt-stellung/?amp=1); contemporaneous coverage at [Marketplace, 17 May 2010](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2010/05/17/groupon-buys-european-competitor)
- After Groupon's 2011 IPO filing, deutsche-startups headlined the deal as: at a $126M valuation, the sellers took out $170M and kept 10.3% of the shares (Groupon stock). — [deutsche-startups.de, 3 Jun 2011](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2011/06/03/grouponcitydeal-bei-126-millionen-dollar-bewertung-170-millionen-dollar-mitnehmen-und-103-der-anteile-behalten/) (headline only seen; read the body before quoting exact terms)
- FACT-CHECK: the May 2010 date is correct. Time from founding to sale was about 5 to 6 months, not 100 days. The price was not disclosed at signing.

**Zalando**
- 2025 results: revenue EUR 12.3bn (+16.8%), GMV EUR 17.6bn (+14.7%), adjusted EBIT EUR 591M (4.8% margin, +15.6%), 62M active customers, run as a multi-app group (Zalando, About You, Lounge). — [Ecotextile, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.ecotextile.com/2026031262026/radar/zalando-reports-record-annual-results/); full report: [Zalando Annual Report 2025 (PDF)](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/media/47/74/795619/zalando-se_annual-report-2025_compressed.pdf)
- 100-day returns: Zalando ended the 100-day return window in Germany, the Netherlands and Italy on 7 Jan 2025 (now 30 days). Spain and France had already moved to 30 days in 2024; Austria and Switzerland were already at 30. — [TextilWirtschaft](https://www.textilwirtschaft.de/business/news/jetzt-auch-in-deutschland-zalando-kippt-100-tage-rueckgabefrist-248524)
- Pay-after-delivery (invoice) was core to the German model. German customers pay after receiving the goods and the invoice, while French customers pay upfront. Insiders said about 4 in 5 shoe orders and 65% of women's jeans came back, and Zalando "completely underestimated" returns. Its slogan invited customers to return anything that didn't make them "cry out loud with happiness" (Schrei vor Glück). — [The Local DE, 30 May 2012](https://www.thelocal.com/20120530/42843)
- FACT-CHECK: "100-day free returns" was historically true but is now outdated in its core markets. Invoice/Rechnung is confirmed. Sofort, the 2008 founding, the 2014 IPO and the warehouses are NOT verified here (see Gaps).

**StudiVZ vs Facebook**
- StudiVZ was founded in October 2005 and launched in November 2005, by Ehssan Dariani and Dennis Bemmann. It had 15M+ members across Europe (mostly DE/CH/AT) by Sept 2009, and traffic declined steadily from late 2009. It was sold to Momentous Entertainment in early 2017 for about 10M, declared insolvent in Sept 2017, and shut down permanently in March 2022. — [Wikipedia mirror: StudiVZ](https://kiwix.lema.org/wikipedia_en_all_mini_2024-02/A/StudiVZ); insolvency coverage: [ComputerBase, Sep 2017](https://www.computerbase.de/2017-09/studivz-betreiber-insolvenz/)
- German press reported that Facebook originally wanted to acquire StudiVZ. — [WinFuture](https://winfuture.de/news,41435.html) (headline seen; body not fetched)

### Inferences
- The Samwer playbook's main structural advantage was speed to a local monopoly before the original arrived. Exit value came from the US incumbent's urgency to enter: eBay bought Alando about 3 months after its launch, and Groupon bought CityDeal about 6 months after its founding. The lesson: when a clone dominates the local market first, the US incumbent's cheapest way in is to buy it.
- Zalando's decisive localization was behavioral: accepting buy-try-return and pay-on-invoice habits in Germany even though it destroyed margins early. Cutting to 30 days once it was the category leader (2024 to 2025) shows the generous policy was a customer-acquisition weapon, not a permanent feature.
- StudiVZ lost because copying a feature set is no defense once the global network localizes (German-language Facebook) and network effects cross borders. Its peak in 2009 and decline from late 2009 line up with that.

### Gaps
- Alando exact price: UNVERIFIED. eBay did not disclose it; "$43M" is widely repeated (some sources say DM ~75M) and needs a primary source.
- Zalando founding (2008, started with flip-flops), Rocket/Samwer funding, Sofort support, Erfurt/Mönchengladbach/Lahr warehouses, Oct 2014 Frankfurt IPO: UNVERIFIED (background knowledge). I did not fetch a source.
- Rocket's "blitzkrieg" email (Oliver Samwer, 2011, leaked via TechCrunch) and failed clones (e.g., Wimdu vs Airbnb, closed 2018; Groupon-clone wars; Home24/Jumia struggles; Lamudi; Foodpanda sold to Delivery Hero): UNVERIFIED in this session.
- Why StudiVZ lost (privacy scandals around 2006, Holtzbrinck buying it for about EUR 85M in Jan 2007, failure to go mobile, Facebook's German launch in 2008): UNVERIFIED (background knowledge).

## Q2. Walmart Germany, Korea, Japan; Tesco Fresh & Easy; Target Canada

### Takeaway
The "smile rule" story is real, but the more credible explanations are structural: a fragmented network bought from two mismatched chains, no price edge against Aldi and Lidl, and a weak economy. Target Canada is the cleanest example of failure through execution and speed.

### Cited Findings
- Walmart entered Germany by buying two "B-level", very different chains, Wertkauf and Interspar. That left far-apart stores, many in poor locations. On 28 July 2006 it announced the sale of its 85 German stores to Metro AG, taking a loss of about $1bn after about 8 years. — [CBS News, Aug 2006](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/wal-marts-german-flop)
- Cultural missteps: required smiling (some male shoppers read it as flirting), a morning company chant, and staff bagging groceries that Germans bag themselves. — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/wal-marts-german-flop)
- The same article argues culture was NOT the main cause. Walmart "was largely unable to undercut its competitors" (Aldi, Lidl, Metro's discounters), the German economy was weak, and the acquired network was fragmented. — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/wal-marts-german-flop)
- Japan: Walmart bought 6% of Seiyu in 2002 and took full ownership in 2008. In Nov 2020 it sold 65% to KKR and 20% to Rakuten, keeping 15%, at a valuation of JPY 172.5bn (about $1.65bn) including debt. That was below the JPY 300 to 500bn it had reportedly sought. Walmart Japan booked its first profit (JPY 47M) in 2019. Walmart withdrew from South Korea in 2006. — [CNBC, 16 Nov 2020](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/11/16/walmart-nearly-exits-japan-after-selling-majority-stake-in-seiyu.html); [Walmart 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416920000068/exhibit991formx11162020.htm)
- Target Canada: in 2011 Target paid HBC $1.8bn for Zellers leases instead of building its own stores. It opened 124 stores in 2013 and had 133 at exit (Jan 2015), with a $5.4bn writedown and about $2bn in net losses. Causes: dumpy, poorly configured inherited locations; stock-outs from opening too fast; prices not sharp enough against Walmart (in Canada since 1994). CEO Brian Cornell: "we were losing money every day." — [Fortune, 15 Jan 2015](https://www.fortune.com/2015/01/15/target-canada-fail)
- Tesco sold Fresh & Easy to Ron Burkle's Yucaipa (Sept 2013), and Tesco lent Yucaipa about GBP 80M to take it on. — [Investegate RNS](https://investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/tesco--tsco/tesco-sells-fresh-easy-to-yucaipa/3250755); [Retail Gazette](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2013/09/42110-tesco-lend-yucaipa-80m-to-take-fresh-easy-off-its-hands/) (headline only)

### Inferences
- Common thread: buying or inheriting a bad store network (Wertkauf/Interspar, Zellers leases) plus no structural price advantage against entrenched local discounters (Aldi/Lidl, Walmart Canada). Cultural gaffes made the failures more visible but didn't decide them.
- Seiyu shows Walmart's playbook (EDLP, scale buying) only worked in Japan once paired with a local digital partner (Rakuten, 2018). That fits a "local partner for the last mile of behavior" lesson.

### Gaps
- Knorr & Arndt (2003), "Why did Wal-Mart fail in Germany?": not fetched. Also unverified: claims that Walmart broke German below-cost-pricing law, the ban on workplace romance in its ethics code, US managers who didn't speak German, and the works-council conflicts.
- Walmart Korea: sold 16 stores to Shinsegae (E-Mart) in 2006 for about $882M. UNVERIFIED (background knowledge); only the 2006 exit is sourced.
- Tesco Fresh & Easy: launched 2007, total cost about GBP 1bn+, small-format/self-checkout mismatch with US habits. UNVERIFIED; the RNS body was not captured.

## Q3. McDonald's abroad: Bolivia, Iceland, India, France, Russia, revenue mix

### Takeaway
McDonald's is now majority international by revenue, which supports "localize per market." But two of the user's claims need correcting. Iceland was a currency and import-cost exit, not a cultural rejection, and its last restaurant closed on 30 Oct 2009. Russia's 850 restaurants were taken over almost one-for-one by a local franchisee and rebranded.

### Cited Findings
- FY2025: total revenue $26,885M. U.S. $10,487M; International Operated Markets $13,410M; International Developmental Licensed Markets & Corporate $2,342M. 45,356 restaurants, about 95% franchised, more than 100 countries. — [McDonald's FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd-20251231.htm)
  - Derived: the non-U.S. segments are about $15.75bn, roughly 60% of segment revenue.
- Iceland: McDonald's opened in Reykjavík in 1993. Franchisee Lyst ehf. announced closure on 26 Oct 2009, and the last branch closed on 30 Oct 2009. Cause: the krona collapsed (EUR 1 cost about ISK 95 in Jan 2008 and over ISK 186 in Oct 2009), everything was bought in euros, and imported farm goods faced high tariffs (e.g., German onions). It reopened on 1 Nov 2009 as Metro with mostly local ingredients, keeping 90 staff. As of 2019 Metro had 2 restaurants. — [Wikipedia: Metro (restaurant chain)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_(restaurant_chain)); [CNN Money, 27 Oct 2009](https://money.cnn.com/2009/10/27/news/international/McDonalds_Iceland.cnnw/index.htm)
- FACT-CHECK: Iceland had 3 restaurants. CNN names the owner as Magnus Ogmundsson. The closure was 30/31 Oct depending on time zone ("closing at midnight on Saturday" 31 Oct). The local-sourcing claim is confirmed.
- Russia: operations were suspended in March 2022, and the full exit was decided about 2 months later. All 850 restaurants went to Alexander Govor (a Siberian franchisee with 25 units), who kept all 62,000 staff. It reopened 12 June 2022 as "Vkusno i Tochka"; deal terms were undisclosed. — [AP via News5 Cleveland](https://www.news5cleveland.com/news/national/russia-ukraine-conflict/russian-owned-successor-of-mcdonalds-opens-in-moscow)
- FACT-CHECK: the buyer is spelled Govor (not Govorov) in AP coverage.
- Bolivia: multiple outlets covered the McDonald's exit and the documentary "¿Por qué quebró McDonald's en Bolivia?" (Fast Food Off the Shelf). — [The World/PRI](https://theworld.org/stories/2016/08/01/mcdonald-s-failure-bolivia); [El Ciudadano](https://www.elciudadano.com/?p=60152); [Bolivian Express](https://bolivianexpress.org/magazine-sub-item/284) (not fetched, titles only)

### Inferences
- The Iceland lesson is structural: an imported-supply-chain model breaks under currency shocks, and a local operator with local sourcing can keep the same assets running. Russia shows the same thing: the brand left, but the operating system (stores, staff, suppliers) stayed and was rebranded.

### Gaps
- Bolivia details (entered 1997, 8 restaurants, all closed 2002): UNVERIFIED. The documentary's cultural explanation (Bolivians distrust fast-prepared food) is contested; price relative to local salteñas and the 2001–02 recession are also cited. Treat as anecdote.
- Russia exit charge (about $1.2–1.4bn reported in 2022): UNVERIFIED in this session.
- India menu (McAloo Tikki, Maharaja Mac made with chicken since 2012, no beef or pork), France McCafé/McBaguette, franchisee-invented items (Filet-O-Fish, Lou Groen, Cincinnati 1962; Egg McMuffin, Herb Peterson, 1971–72): UNVERIFIED (background knowledge). McDonald's own history pages would confirm these.

## Q4. iFood vs Uber Eats/Rappi (Brazil)

### Takeaway
iFood won Brazil through local density plus restaurant exclusivity. Uber Eats left restaurant delivery on 7 March 2022, and CADE then capped iFood's exclusivity in Feb 2023, which is how the regulator responded to the local winner's lock-in.

### Cited Findings
- Uber Eats stopped restaurant delivery in Brazil on 7 March 2022 and kept other delivery services. Rappi described Uber Eats as the #2 player. Rappi and Uber had complained to CADE about iFood's exclusivity contracts. — [Bloomberg Línea, Jan 2022](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/ifood-and-rappi-bid-farewell-to-uber-eats-in-brazil/)
- CADE settlement (Feb 2023): no more than 25% of iFood's partner restaurants may be under exclusivity. There's no exclusivity for brands with 30+ locations, new exclusivity is capped at 2 years, older long contracts go into a 1-year quarantine, and the agreement runs 4 years with an independent monitor. Rappi CEO Tijana Jankovic welcomed it. — [Correio Braziliense, Feb 2023](https://www.correiobraziliense.com.br/economia/2023/02/5072890-ifood-assina-acordo-que-restringe-exclusividade-com-restaurantes.html)
- Academic analysis of exclusivity in Brazilian food delivery. — [Kira, "Starving the market", UCL, Mar 2023 (PDF)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10173585/1/Starving%20the%20market%20-%20Beatriz%20Kira%20-%20March%202023.pdf)
- The Irish Times reported iFood "corners meals market." — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/innovation/brazilian-delivery-group-ifood-corners-meals-market-1.4827443) (headline only)

### Gaps
- iFood market share (often cited as 80%+) and current order volumes: not verified on a fetched page.

## Q5. Mercado Libre vs eBay/Amazon

### Takeaway
MELI beat the global players by owning payments (Mercado Pago) and logistics (Envios) in markets where both were broken. eBay chose to be a passive 19.5% shareholder instead of competing.

### Cited Findings
- In Oct 2001 MercadoLibre took over iBazar's Brazil unit, and eBay received a 19.5% stake instead of cash. On 13 Oct 2016 MELI announced a secondary offering of up to 5.5M shares (plus 825k over-allotment) sold by eBay. — [PYMNTS, Oct 2016](https://www.pymnts.com/news/ecommerce/2016/ebay-unloading-stake-in-mercadolibre/)
- Q2 2026: net revenue $10,169M (+50% YoY, +43% FX-neutral), net income $466M (-11%, 4.6% margin), GMV $21.9B (+44%), TPV $101B (+56%), 89M unique active buyers, 88M fintech MAU (+30%), credit portfolio over $16B (+75%). Brazil revenue $5,530M (+59%), Mexico $2,337M (+55%), Argentina $1,839M (+20% USD, +48% FX-neutral). — [MELI Q2 2026 earnings release (SEC 8-K)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001099590/000109959026000021/meli-20260805xex991.htm)
- Margin compression came alongside heavy investment (free-shipping threshold, credit). — [Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001099590/000109959026000023/meli-20260630.htm)

### Inferences
- eBay's 2001 decision to swap its Brazil unit for equity let it profit as an investor but handed MELI the region. Kaspi and Nubank show the same pattern: payments and credit first, which commerce players coming from mature markets underweight.

### Gaps
- Q3 2026 results (due early Nov 2026) were not available on the pages I fetched. Current market cap not verified.
- Mercado Pago founding (2003) and Mercado Envios (2013): UNVERIFIED dates.
