# China and Russia: Local Players vs Global Giants (decisions, sequencing, fact-check)

Scope note: research conducted Nov 1 2026. Latest company data available: Yum China Q2 2026 (Q3 2026 not yet released as of this date) and FY2025 10-K; McDonald's China data as of Nov 2025; Yandex share as of Apr 2026. Several English Wikipedia pages were blocked by the fetch allowlist; a Wikipedia mirror was used once for VK (marked). Claims not verified in a fetched source are listed in Gaps, not stated as fact.

## 1. VKontakte vs Facebook (Russia)

### Takeaway
VK won mainly on PRODUCT and timing: it launched in Oct 2006 as a Russian-language Facebook clone, hit 10M users before Facebook even had a Russian interface (2008), and kept a ~4-5x user lead into 2011. Piracy-tolerant media was a real growth lever. State pressure shaped VK's ownership later (Durov ousted 2014), but it was not why VK beat Facebook; that race was decided by 2008-2011.

### Cited Findings
**Timeline and numbers**
- Beta Sept 2006; official launch Oct 10 2006; Durov had just graduated from St Petersburg State University; company incorporated Jan 19 2007 — [VK article, Wikipedia mirror](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Vk.com.html)
- Registration initially "limited to within university circles exclusively by invitation" — [same](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Vk.com.html)
- Users: 100k (Feb 2007), 1M (Jul 2007), 10M (Apr 2008); overtook Odnoklassniki as Russia's top social network Dec 2008; 369M+ accounts by Jun 2016 — [same](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Vk.com.html)
- Facebook launched its Russian interface in 2008 and started active marketing only in 2010; by late Apr 2011 it had 5M active Russian users (~1 in 10 Runet users) vs VKontakte 23M unique users (TNS, Feb 2011) and Odnoklassniki 18M — [EWDN, 2011](https://ewdn.com/?p=2907)
- EWDN describes VK as launched in 2006 as a Facebook "clone" — [EWDN](https://ewdn.com/?p=2907)

**Piracy / copyright**
- 2008: RTR sued over unlicensed films; Supreme Arbitration Court ruled for VK in 2010 (not liable for user uploads). 2012: court partly sided with Gala Records ($7,000 fine). Oct 2013: St Petersburg court cleared VK. Nov 2014: regulator said VK would finish legalizing content in early 2015 — [Wikipedia mirror](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Vk.com.html)

**Ownership / state**
- Founders sold 39.99% to Mail.ru Group (then DST); Apr 2013 UCP bought 48% for $1.12B; Jan 2014 Durov sold his 12%; Apr 21 2014 Durov dismissed as CEO, said company was taken over by Putin allies after he refused to hand over user data, then left Russia; Sept 16 2014 Mail.ru bought UCP's 48% for $1.5B and became sole owner — [Wikipedia mirror](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Vk.com.html) (note: the page's account of the Jan 2014 buyer is internally inconsistent)

2. 2007-2008: scale from 1M to 10M users in 9 months while Facebook had no Russian UI — [mirror](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Vk.com.html), [EWDN](https://ewdn.com/?p=2907)
3. 2008-2013: tolerate user-uploaded music/film and fight lawsuits, winning the key 2010 liability ruling — [mirror](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Vk.com.html)
4. Dec 2008: overtake Odnoklassniki (the other local rival) — [mirror](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Vk.com.html)

**What Facebook got wrong:** localized ~2 years late (2008) and marketed ~4 years late (2010); relied on invite-driven growth in a market where VK already owned the graph — [EWDN](https://ewdn.com/?p=2907)

### Inferences
- The cleanest lesson: in network-effect markets, the first credible local-language version captures the graph; a superior global product arriving 2 years later cannot dislodge it. Speed of localization beat product quality.
- Piracy was a structural-legal advantage (weak IP enforcement) dressed as a product feature: VK offered free music/film that a US-listed company could not.
- Government protection was NOT the main reason VK beat Facebook (Facebook was never blocked in Russia during 2006-2014). State influence shows up later in ownership control.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for VK being lighter/faster, for morphological search, or for SMS as a growth driver (dates). Durov's specific growth hacks (e.g. targeting top students, contests) not sourced in this pass.
- Facebook's share after the Mar 2022 Russian block (state ban) not researched; that later outcome IS structural.

## 2. Yandex vs Google (Russia)

### Takeaway
Yandex held off Google mainly on PRODUCT (Russian morphology from day one, regional relevance, local services like real-time traffic) plus Google's late commitment (first Russian office 2006). Google climbed to ~18-22% by 2010 then stalled; Yandex is at ~70% in 2026, with post-2022 geopolitics an added structural tailwind.

### Cited Findings
- Yandex was built for Russian grammar from the start; Google added morphology-based Russian search only in 2006 — [EWDN, May 2011](https://ewdn.com/?p=3380)
- Google launched Russian-language search in 2001 but opened its first Russian office only in 2006 — [EWDN](https://ewdn.com/?p=3380)
- Mid-2000s: Google rose from 6% to 18% while Yandex fell from 60% to 51% ("It was really scary," Volozh); after 2008 Google stopped gaining despite more marketing; 2010 Google fell 1.9 pts to 21.5% — [EWDN](https://ewdn.com/?p=3380)
- Yandex launched real-time traffic four years before Google offered similar in Russia; improved regional search; launched Matrixnet ranking in Dec 2009 — [EWDN](https://ewdn.com/?p=3380)
- Yandex at ~70% of Russian search queries (Apr 2026), Google ~30%; Yandex credits thematic search, object answers, Alice AI — [bne IntelliNews, Apr 2026](https://www.intellinews.com/yandex-captures-70-of-russia-s-search-market-outpacing-google-436161/)
- Yandex's Dutch parent sold the Russian business in Feb 2024 for RUB475bn ($5.2bn) — [bne IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/yandex-captures-70-of-russia-s-search-market-outpacing-google-436161/)
- Methodology disputes exist on share data (e.g. a Morgan Stanley claim that Google surpassed Yandex was challenged) — [EWDN](https://ewdn.com/?p=34614)

**Decisive decisions:** (1) build for Russian morphology from day one; (2) regional search relevance; (3) local services before Google (traffic); (4) Dec 2009 Matrixnet ranking overhaul — all [EWDN](https://ewdn.com/?p=3380)

### Inferences
- Google's mid-2000s surge shows a global product CAN win share when the local leader stagnates; Yandex reversed it by out-investing on language quality and local data services. Mostly PRODUCT, not protection, through 2014.
- Post-2014 and especially post-2022, structural factors (Android/FAS case, Western exits) likely matter more; not verified here.

### Gaps
- The 2015-2017 FAS antitrust ruling forcing Google to unbundle search on Android, and Google Russia's 2022 subsidiary bankruptcy, were not verified in fetched sources.

## 3. Taobao vs eBay/EachNet (China)

### Takeaway
A PRODUCT and trust win: Taobao went free, built escrow (Alipay) and buyer-seller chat (AliWangWang) for a low-trust market, while eBay imposed fees, a US layout, and San Jose control. eBay EachNet went from ~80% share at acquisition to ~20% by 2006 and handed China to a Tom Online JV in Dec 2006. No meaningful government protection involved.

### Cited Findings
- eBay entered via EachNet in 2002 (initial deal, per ICMR) — [ICMR](https://icmrindia.org/Casestudies/catalogue/Business%20Strategy/ebay's-problems-in-china-case.htm); Wharton dates entry to 2003 (full acquisition) when EachNet "controlled nearly 80% of the online auction sector" — [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=6022)
- eBay's share fell each year to ~20% in 2006; Taobao ~70% of C2C by users; Taobao >$1.2B annual transactions vs eBay ~$600M in 2005; eBay invested ~$300M in China by end-2006 — [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=6022)
- Taobao claimed >70% of C2C three years after eBay's entry — [TechNode](https://technode.com/2013/05/10/taobao-turns-ten-a-look-back/)
- When eBay entered, Taobao waived all store fees and Jack Ma told staff not to aim for profit for three years — [TechNode](https://technode.com/2013/05/10/taobao-turns-ten-a-look-back/)
- Alipay launched Oct 2003 with escrow (funds held until buyer confirms receipt); Wang Wang IM launched Jul 2004 — [TechNode](https://technode.com/2013/05/10/taobao-turns-ten-a-look-back/)
- eBay initially blocked direct buyer-seller communication; PayPal lacked escrow; US-style "empty" layout; no customer service phone number; ex-EachNet execs felt San Jose didn't listen and many left — [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=6022)
- eBay went free on listings in early 2006 and still lost users; a CMR survey found fees were not in the top 5 reasons consumers chose Taobao — [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=6022)
- Dec 20 2006 TOM eBay JV: eBay 49% / Tom Online 51%; eBay contributed $40M + EachNet; Tom $20M + local management — [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=6022); Whitman called it an "evolution" not defeat — [ICMR](https://icmrindia.org/Casestudies/catalogue/Business%20Strategy/ebay's-problems-in-china-case.htm)

**Fact-check of user's notes**
| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| EachNet ~85% share | PARTLY: best source found says "nearly 80%" ([Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=6022)); ICMR says "more than half until 2005" ([ICMR](https://icmrindia.org/Casestudies/catalogue/Business%20Strategy/ebay's-problems-in-china-case.htm)). Use ~80%. |
| Decline | TRUE: to ~20% by 2006 ([Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=6022)) |
| Free listings for 3 years | TRUE in substance: fees waived, "no profit for three years" directive ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2013/05/10/taobao-turns-ten-a-look-back/)) |
| AliWangWang | TRUE, Jul 2004 ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2013/05/10/taobao-turns-ten-a-look-back/)) |
| Alipay escrow | TRUE, Oct 2003 ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2013/05/10/taobao-turns-ten-a-look-back/)) |
| eBay moved servers to US platform | UNVERIFIED in fetched sources (widely reported 2004 migration; not confirmed here) |
| eBay exit / Tom Online JV 2006 | TRUE, Dec 20 2006 ([Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=6022)) |

### Inferences
- Sequencing: trust infrastructure (escrow, chat) mattered more than price; eBay's late switch to free listings in 2006 did not recover share, which suggests price was table stakes and trust was the moat.
- The governance failure (HQ overriding local execs) is the most transferable lesson for global entrants.

### Gaps
- Exact 2004 platform migration date/impact not verified. Paipai (Tencent) share not quantified.

## 4. Uber China vs Didi (2014-2016)

### Takeaway
Mostly a capital-and-distribution war that Didi won through local super-app distribution and deeper funding; Uber burned ~$1B/year and sold in Aug 2016 for 5.89% equity / 17.7% economic interest. Structural element: Didi's backers (Tencent, Alibaba) controlled the payment and messaging rails.

### Cited Findings
- Uber losing ~$1B a year in China (Kalanick, Feb 2016) — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2016/02/18/uber-losing-1-billion-a-year-in-china.html)
- Aug 1 2016: Didi buys Uber China; Uber gets 5.89% stake with preferential equity equal to 17.7% economic interest; Baidu (an Uber China investor) gets 2.3%; Cheng Wei joins Uber board, Kalanick joins Didi board — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/01/didi-chuxing-confirms-it-is-buying-ubers-business-in-china/)
- Valuations: Uber China ~$7B (Jan 2016), Didi $28B (Jun 2016); Didi claimed 15M drivers, 300M users; Uber China >150M trips/month — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/01/didi-chuxing-confirms-it-is-buying-ubers-business-in-china/)
- Kalanick: "Getting to profitability is the only way to build a sustainable business" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/01/didi-chuxing-confirms-it-is-buying-ubers-business-in-china/)
- Bloomberg reported Didi invests $1B in Uber global (unconfirmed by TechCrunch) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/01/didi-chuxing-confirms-it-is-buying-ubers-business-in-china/)

**Fact-check:** "Uber China sale for ~17.7%-20% of Didi" — TRUE as 17.7% economic interest (5.89% voting/equity). "20%" is an overstatement. Subsidy war: $1B/yr Uber loss TRUE; Didi subsidy totals not verified.

### Inferences
- Didi's edge (WeChat/Alipay payment and in-app entry from investors Tencent/Alibaba) is a distribution advantage the global player could not buy. Classify as STRUCTURAL-commercial (ecosystem), not government protection.
- Uber's exit was a rational capital decision, not a product loss: it kept 17.7% economics of the winner.

### Gaps
- Didi-Kuaidi merger (Feb 2015), Didi subsidy figures, market share (~85-90% commonly cited), and the July 2016 ride-hailing legalization rules were not verified in fetched sources.

## 5. Meituan vs Groupon clones; Meituan-Dianping (2015); Ele.me/Alibaba

### Takeaway
PRODUCT/execution win: in the "Thousand Group-Buying War" (5,000+ sites, Mar 2010-Aug 2011) Meituan refused the TV ad war, hoarded cash, and built an Alibaba-style ground sales team; rivals died from ad burn and failed IPOs. It then merged with Dianping (Oct 2015) with Tencent backing, and fought Alibaba-owned Ele.me in delivery.

### Cited Findings
- Meituan launched Mar 4 2010; Mar 2010-Aug 2011 China had "over 5,000 group-buying sites" (超过5000家团购网站) — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725210902529)
- 2011 ad war: Tuangou 550M yuan (CCTV, Hunan TV, Focus Media), Nuomi 200M, Dianping 300-400M; Meituan was the only major player that did not join — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725210902529)
- Wang Xing adopted "no brand ads, strong offline operations" after advice from ex-Alibaba president Guan Mingsheng; ground team built by ex-Alibaba sales leader Gan Jiawei ("狂拜访、狂上单"); Wang Xing's earlier forced sale of Xiaonei over funding made Meituan cash-flow obsessed — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725210902529)
- Funding: Meituan $12M Sequoia (late 2010); Lashou $160M across 3 rounds; rivals died via Lashou's subsidies/failed IPO and Wowotuan's unpaid suppliers — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725210902529)
- Oct 2015 Meituan-Dianping merger, co-CEOs; Tencent led ~$1B round; Alibaba reportedly given 12 hours' notice — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725210902529)
- Ele.me (founded 2008) was early delivery leader; refused Meituan's acquisition approach; Alibaba later bought it for $9.5B — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725210902529)

**Fact-check:** "5,000 group-buying sites" — TRUE (cumulative over Mar 2010-Aug 2011, per 36Kr; not a single-day peak).

### Inferences
- Wang Xing's sequencing: survive first (cash), win supply side city by city (ground team), then expand into high-frequency categories (food delivery) and merge for capital. The "frequency" logic (food delivery as daily-use wedge) is consistent with this but not sourced explicitly here.
- No government protection; Groupon's China JV (Gaopeng) loss was execution.

### Gaps
- Groupon/Gaopeng entry (2011) details and failure, Wang Xing's explicit unit-economics quotes, Meituan's current delivery share vs Alibaba/JD (2025-2026 delivery war) not verified.

## 6. Trip.com (Ctrip)

### Cited Findings
- Ctrip founded 1999; operates a "centralized, toll-free, 24-hour customer service center"; describes itself as the "largest online consolidator of accommodations and transportation tickets in China" — [Ctrip 6-K, Oct 2015](https://ctripcominternationalltd.gcs-web.com/static-files/453a034f-6062-469d-a845-c67d56bf5586)

### Inferences
- Call centers fit a market where online card payment and trust were weak; Ctrip consolidated rivals rather than out-competing them indefinitely.

### Gaps

## 7. WeChat vs WhatsApp/Kik/Line (China) and WeChat's failure abroad

### Takeaway
In China WeChat won on PRODUCT plus a huge distribution head start (QQ's 750M MAU at launch) and a payments masterstroke (2014 red envelopes). Abroad it failed because it was a China-designed product with cosmetic localization arriving after WhatsApp's network effects. STRUCTURAL note: WhatsApp/Line were later blocked in China, but WeChat had already won.

### Cited Findings
- QQ had >750M MAU when WeChat launched (2010/2011); WeChat hit 100M global users in 433 days — [FactorDaily](https://archive.factordaily.com/how-wechat-faded-into-the-silence-in-india/)
- Red envelopes: 16M sent in 2014; 1B in 2015; Tencent paid $7.7M for the 2015 CCTV gala, ~11B phone shakes over 5 hours; 8B envelopes in 2016; Jack Ma called it a "Pearl Harbor"-like attack; Alibaba paid $41M for 2016 gala rights — [IMD](https://www.imd.org/research-knowledge/articles/the-uneasy-truce-between-alibaba-and-tencent-is-over/)
- India push: team assembled in Gurgaon early 2012; first mobile app with Indian TV ads; Parineeti Chopra and Varun Dhawan ambassadors; ~20-25M sign-ups; #1 on Google Play for 45 days — [FactorDaily](https://archive.factordaily.com/how-wechat-faded-into-the-silence-in-india/)
- Why it faded: mandatory "add friend" vs WhatsApp's contact-book model; "people nearby" on by default led to harassment of women; ~40MB app on phones with <200MB free; no media compression; Symbian/BlackBerry builds 1.5+ years late; localization "mostly cosmetic"; 2013 ban rumours; focused on Line not WhatsApp — [FactorDaily](https://archive.factordaily.com/how-wechat-faded-into-the-silence-in-india/)

**Fact-check:** "India/SEA push around 2013-2014" — PARTLY: India campaign started early 2012 per FactorDaily; 2013 is when Line ran TV ads. Red envelopes 2014 CNY — TRUE.

### Inferences
- The same China advantages (QQ graph, payments rails) were non-transferable; WeChat abroad was the "global giant arriving late" and lost for the same reasons Facebook lost Russia.

### Gaps
- Mini Programs launch (Jan 2017) and WeChat Pay share vs Alipay not verified this pass.

### Inferences
- KFC's win is product/ops localization by a global brand run locally. No protection involved.

### Gaps

## 10. Home Depot China (2006-2012)

### Takeaway
Pure PRODUCT/culture mismatch: a DIY big-box model in a "do-it-for-me" market with cheap labor.

### Cited Findings
- Entered by buying 12 Home Way stores in six cities (~2006); closed last seven big boxes Sept 13 2012; kept two Tianjin specialty stores and 170 sourcing staff — [AJC/AP, Sept 2012](https://www.ajc.com/business/diy-doesn-fly-china-home-depot-learns/IVDkRQaYhvHG5F5yof6ByN)
- Reasons: Chinese consumers hire out work; cheap labor; high-rise living; "There is no real do-it-yourself mentality in China" (Steve Kirn) — [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/business/diy-doesn-fly-china-home-depot-learns/IVDkRQaYhvHG5F5yof6ByN)

### Inferences
- Lesson: validate the core behavior (who does the job) before format; a DIFM service model was needed.

### Gaps

## Cross-case classification (inference)
- Mainly PRODUCT/behavioral: VK vs Facebook (pre-2014), Yandex vs Google (pre-2014), Taobao vs eBay, Meituan, Ctrip, Home Depot failure, KFC win.
- Mixed (ecosystem/capital): Didi vs Uber.
- Mainly GOVERNMENT/censorship (after the fact): Baidu post-2010, WeChat's lock-in after WhatsApp/Line blocks, VK ownership after 2014, Yandex after 2022.
- Recurring winning sequence: localize first in your language/behavior, build trust infrastructure (escrow, call centers, ground teams), conserve cash while rivals burn on ads, then consolidate via mergers.
